# Skill Mix: plan

## The idea

The child presses ▶️ and plays short **blocks** of one game at a time: 3–4 questions, then a different game. Every block opens with the game's intro and a **demo** where the hand plays one example, then "Your turn!". Every game starts at its easiest level. Later, each game's own record (tries, right answers, mistakes, time) moves it up or down, so each skill stays just hard enough on its own.

## Who it's for right now

A 3-year-old who:
- counts to 10 and recites numbers to 19;
- reads numerals to 9 with about 60–70% accuracy;
- doesn't yet have a sense that 10 is more than 5.

He needs a nudge to tap, finds a mouse hard, and loses interest quickly. The aim is 5 minutes at first, growing to 15+ minutes over a few weeks.

What that means for the design (and the research behind it):

| Finding | Design response |
|---|---|
| Knowing that the last number counted is "how many" (cardinality) typically arrives around 3–4 (Wynn; Sarnecka). Reciting numbers comes long before. | Counting by **tapping each animal** is the main way in. When every animal is counted, the matching number card lights up. |
| A sense of which amount is bigger is built from comparing quantities, and from seeing numbers laid out in order (Siegler & Ramani's number-board studies). | **Which has more?** is a core game: food plates with big differences (at least 2×, up to 10), laid out in rows of 5 so longer means more. A linear number-board game is a strong candidate next. |
| Recognising small amounts at a glance (subitizing) is a foundation of number sense (Clements & Sarama). | Numbers 1–5 always come with dots (in the question and on the cards). The dots will fade at higher levels. |
| Toddlers solve tiny additions when they *see the action* (Huttenlocher, Jordan & Levine). Symbols come years later. | Adding (sums up to 5) shows the second group **walking over** to join the first, then he counts them all. |
| Hearing the first sound of a word and linking it to a letter usually develops around 4–5, and only with spoken prompts (National Early Literacy Panel). | **First letter** and **Starts with…** are paused for now. |
| 3-year-olds struggle to switch rules from one task to the next (Zelazo's card-sorting studies). | **Blocks of 3–4** questions per game instead of a new game every question, and a demo at each switch. |
| Preschoolers point poorly with a mouse (Hourcade). Toddlers often rest a palm or touch with several fingers. | **Touch first.** An answer counts when the finger **lifts** on the same card. Palms, several fingers at once, or sliding off the card pick nothing. |
| Showing children exactly what to do reduces their exploring (Bonawitz et al.). Feedback that shows *why* teaches more than right/wrong (Fyfe & Rittle-Johnson). | **Mistakes stay and are explained.** A wrong card is never taken away. The game shows what the pick means (below), and only after 3 tries does the right card pulse (glow after 5). It never answers for him. |
| Children learn more from screens with an adult alongside (joint media engagement; AAP guidance for ages 2–5). | A **grown-up tip** shows during each demo. **Session length** (5 / 8 / 10 / 15 min) ends on a trophy while he still wants more. |

## How each game explains a mistake

| Game | What he sees after a wrong pick |
|---|---|
| Find the number | The dots of the number he picked line up under the question's dots, so the extra or missing ones show. |
| How many? / Add them up | The number he picked pairs up with the animals: an orange dot under each one it covers, red dots for "too many", and the animals it leaves out pulse. |
| Which has more? | The two plates pair up one to one, and the leftovers on the bigger plate light up. |
| What comes next? | His colour drops into the gap and the pattern visibly breaks (shake, red border), then pops back out. |
| Same shape | The shape he picked is laid over the target, so the mismatch shows. |
| Spell the word | The tile jumps at the empty slot and bounces off (it doesn't fit). |

## Status notes

- **Voice:** the iPad's voice was silent because Safari only allows speech that starts inside a tap, and the app spoke ~1 second later (after the intro). The app now starts speech on the ▶️ tap, and avoids a cancel-then-speak race that can drop sentences. **Grown-ups → Test voice** shows what the device's voice is doing.
- **Paused:** First letter, Starts with… and Taking away.

## Rules for "no reading needed"

1. **Show, then do.** Every game opens with a demo: the hand plays an example, then "Your turn!".
2. **Each game has a look and a sound.** Mascot, colour, icon, jingle and a 2–3 word description.
3. **One layout.** The question sits at the top (❓ where the answer goes), with big answer cards at the bottom.
4. **Voice where it works.** Questions are spoken, and 🔊 repeats them. Nothing depends on the voice.
5. **Matching before naming.** Recognition starts as "find the same one".
6. **The same symbols everywhere.** ➕ means joining, ❓ means the answer goes here.
7. **Mistakes are part of playing.** Wrong picks stay tappable and are explained. Hints build slowly and never answer for him.
8. **Progress without numbers.** A sticker row fills up, and 5 stickers bring a party.
9. **Grown-ups are hidden.** Press and hold ⚙️.

## Difficulty ladders

Each step changes **one thing**: more choices, a bigger range, look-alike distractors, a dropped crutch (dots, ghost letters), or tapping → typing. ✅ = built.

| Skill | L1 ✅ | L2 | L3 | L4 | L5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Numbers 🦉 | Find 1–5, 2 choices, dots under numerals and in the question | 3 choices, 1–9, dots only on the question | 4 choices, 0–9, no dots | Look-alikes (6/9, 1/7, 2/5) | Teens (12 vs 21), 10–20 |
| First letter 🐝 (paused) | Picture → pick its first letter, 2 capitals (A B D F M P S T, no look-alike pairs) | 3 capitals, more letters | 4 capitals, A–Z | Look-alike capitals (B/P, E/F, M/N) | Lowercase letters |
| Counting 🐿️ | 1–6 in a row, tap to count, 2 choices with dots | 1–10, 3 choices | 1–10 in dice / ten-frame patterns | 1–10 scattered | Timed, or up to 20 in ten-frames |
| Starts with… 🦜 (paused) | Letter + sound, pick 1 of 2 pictures (A B D F M P S T) | 3 pictures, more letters | Reverse: picture → pick its letter | Similar sounds (B/P, M/N, S/Z) | "Tap all that start with S" |
| Adding 🐻 | Sums up to 5, groups visibly join, 2 choices | Up to 7, 3 choices | Up to 10 | Up to 18 | Multi-digit |
| Taking away 🐰 (paused) | From up to 3, 2 choices | From up to 5, 3 choices | From up to 10 | Up to 18 − 9 | Multi-digit |
| Word jumble 🐙 | 3 letters, ghost letters, 10 words | Only the first ghost letter | No ghosts | 4-letter words (FISH, FROG) | 5 letters or a decoy tile |
| Which has more? 🐘 | Food plates, at least 2× more, up to 10 | Closer amounts (5 vs 7) | Numerals with dots | Numerals only (which is bigger: 4 or 9?) | Order three numbers |
| What comes next 🐛 | AB colours, 2 choices | AB animals, 3 choices | AAB / ABB | ABC | Number patterns (2, 4, 6, ❓) |
| Shapes 🦊 | Match the same shape, 2 choices | Same shape at a different size or turn | Voice: "Find the triangle" | More shapes (oval, hexagon, diamond) | Count the sides |

## New skill areas at higher levels

Beyond harder versions of the ten skills, new areas unlock as the child levels up. Candidates, easiest first (to be chosen together):

| Area | Level 1 idea |
|---|---|
| Big and small | "Which is bigger?" (two sizes of the same animal) |
| Colours by name | "Find blue!" (colour names, not matching) |
| Opposites | Hot / cold, up / down pictures |
| Rhyming | "Which one rhymes with cat?" (🎩 or 🐶) |
| Memory match | Flip two cards to find a pair (4 cards) |
| Odd one out | 3 fruits and 1 car: tap the one that doesn't belong |
| Where is it? | "Find the cat **under** the table" (positions) |
| Sight words | "the", "and", "I" (much later) |
| Skip counting | 2, 4, 6… (much later) |
| Telling time | Hour hands only (much later) |

## Automatic levelling (next iteration)

Each skill already records `{ level, tries, correct, firstTry, totalMs, misses, taps (left/right), recentMisses }` on the device. Proposed rules, to be tuned:

- **Level up** after 3 right-first-time in a row, each within about 8 seconds.
- **Level down** after 2 wrong-first-time in a row. Never below 1.
- **Pick weaker skills a bit more often.** Weight each block by how rarely the game has been right first time recently.
- **Watch for a side habit.** If he picks the same side (left or right) far more than half the time, his right answers may be luck. Hold the level until the habit fades.
- **Unlock a new skill area** once most skills are at level 3 or above.
